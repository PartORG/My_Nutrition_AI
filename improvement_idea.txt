Your app's edge

  Most competitors are cloud apps. Yours runs the AI fully on the phone, works offline, has no account, and supports health conditions and a doctor report. Very few
  apps combine those, so new features should strengthen this position rather than copy cloud features.

  The strongest point across the sources is that people quit tracking mostly because logging takes too many taps. The clearest gaps I found in your app are about
  exactly that.

  2. Home screen (Today) UI

  - One summary ring for calories, with small protein/carbs/fat rings beside it. It's denser than the current bars and is the pattern Yazio, Lifesum and MacroFactor
    use.
  - A "next action" card: "No lunch logged yet: log it or get an idea". It uses the meal times your app already learns.
  - Dark mode: the app has no dark theme at all, and users expect it.
  - Android home-screen widget: today's kcal plus a "Log" button. Packages like glance_widget let you build it in Dart, but they're community packages, so check them
    first.
  - Dynamic colour (Material You) from the wallpaper. Built into Flutter, a one-line change.
  - Empty first day: the first day has empty charts everywhere. Show a short "log your first meal" walkthrough instead.

  4. Data quality (from the earlier database checks)

  - Newer Open Food Facts dump: your packaged-product data is from 2019, which is why European products are missing. A current dump would fix name search, and would
    let barcode scanning come back later.
  - More ingredient mappings: 232,000 recipes are still blocked by unmatched ingredients. The build now lists the top offenders, so adding mappings gradually brings
    recipes back.
  - Medical data: waits for your PDFs, as agreed.

  My recommended order

  1. Logging friction: grams on the chat card, recent/frequent foods, repeat meal, edit meal.
  2. Today screen: dark mode, dynamic colour, rings, next-action card.
  3. Water tracking and fasting timer: small, and expected by users.
  4. Meal plan and shopping list: builds on the ratings work.
  5. Health Connect and the newer Open Food Facts data.
  6. Micronutrients and more languages: the larger efforts.

  Which of these should I start with? I'd begin with group 1. I can also put this research on a shareable page if you want to keep it.
